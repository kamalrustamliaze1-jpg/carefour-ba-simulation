# 11 · Final BA Report

## Executive Summary

Carrefour, with 15,719 stores, ~300,000 employees, and direct operations in 7 countries, is Europe's leading retailer, generating €82.1 billion in net sales and €7.0 billion in e-commerce GMV (+21% year-over-year) in 2025. The company aims to grow e-commerce GMV to €10 billion by 2026 with a €3 billion digital investment, alongside a shift toward an AI-driven operating model. This BA simulation shows that the current (As-Is) sales reporting process is manual, delayed, and imprecise in how it handles returns — out of step with the pace of Carrefour's announced digital transformation.

## Key Findings

- Revenue distribution across loyalty segments is uneven (Basic and Platinum lead, Gold is lowest), but Gold's 23% margin signals its strategic importance.
- Bakery and Dairy are the top-revenue categories both overall and within the Gold segment — an opportunity for cross-sell and shelf-placement strategy.
- Keeping returns management separate from the main report negatively affects revenue accuracy and needs to be addressed with a formal business rule (BR-01).
- The current manual process (2–3 week delay) isn't sustainable at Carrefour's scale (15,000+ stores) and requires a shift to automated, omnichannel data infrastructure.

## Recommendations

| # | Recommendation | Priority | Expected Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Apply the returns-filtering rule (BR-01) across all reporting systems | High | Improved revenue accuracy |
| 2 | Build an omnichannel data warehouse (store + e-commerce) | High | Near real-time decision-making |
| 3 | Launch a dedicated retention campaign for the Gold segment | Medium | Increased segment revenue |
| 4 | Build an automated reconciliation and anomaly alert system | High | Improved data reliability |
| 5 | Introduce cross-sell offers for Bakery/Dairy categories | Medium | Larger basket size |
